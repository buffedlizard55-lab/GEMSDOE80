### Why the cited winner might work

The winning artifact is **not all zeros**. Its owner audit describes 37,654 binary
positive pixels, derived from an earlier sparse base by removing dots within 2 pixels
of catalogue faults. `zeros` denotes the exterior encoding. Removing already mapped
fault predictions can reduce false-positive mass when the target is undiscovered faults;
sparse placement can avoid repeatedly spending prediction mass on the same neighborhood.
These are mechanistic explanations, **not a causal attribution proven by ablation**.
Pruning also removes true nearby continuations, so it is not universally beneficial.
